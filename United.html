STAGE 06

UNITED — NAVIGATION APP

★ MY ROLE

During the Microsoft Design Challenge 22, I addressed accessibility challenges for Acid Attack Survivors (AAS). Over two weeks I designed a navigation app called “UNITED” to help AAS individuals find nearby AAS-friendly locations.

★ PROTOTYPE
★ BRIEF

I conceptualised UNITED — a software application designed to empower AAS individuals in finding nearby AAS-friendly locations including hospitals, rehabilitation centres, support groups, counselling services, and beauty salons.

★ PROBLEM STATEMENT
  • Growing Numbers: The AAS population is increasing daily, highlighting urgency.
  • Inadequate Resources: Lack of awareness and dedicated initiatives for AAS-specific needs.
  • Social Stigma: Pressure of discrimination results in AAS individuals covering their faces, limiting social navigation.
  • Lack of Support: Feelings of isolation and exclusion due to lack of understanding from society.
  • Difficulty Accessing AAS-Friendly Places: Struggle to find accommodating healthcare facilities and support services.
★ GOALS
  • Accessibility: User-friendly app to find nearby AAS-friendly places easily.
  • Promote Acceptance: Reduce social stigmas and encourage understanding.
  • Empower Survivors: Tools and resources to regain confidence and independence.
  • Provide Information: Comprehensive details about AAS-friendly places and support networks.
  • Cultivate Community: Facilitate connections and shared experiences among survivors.
★ USER RESEARCH

I explored platforms including Twitter, Instagram threads and news articles to gather information about AAS challenges and deep-dive into their specific needs.

★ INTERVIEWS & SURVEYS

Through interviews and surveys we gained insights into limited access to AAS-friendly locations, societal stigmas, and lack of support services.

★ USER INSIGHTS & APP FEATURES

Research phase focused on understanding needs and preferences of acid attack survivors to inform development of the UNITED app.

★ COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

Evaluated similar applications in the market to identify key findings and opportunities for differentiation.

★ IDEATION
  • AAS-Friendly Directory: Comprehensive directory of AAS-friendly locations.
  • Ratings & Reviews: Users share experiences and feedback on locations.
  • Safety Measures: Emergency contacts, panic buttons, location sharing.
  • Community Support: Discussion forums and chat groups for connection.
  • Educational Resources: Info about acid attacks, prevention, and support services.
★ NAME & LOGO
★ STORYBOARD

A storyboard designed to visually represent the user experience, map the user journey and identify areas for improvement.

★ USER FLOW

Based on user research, competitive analysis, and storyboarding, a user flow was created to cater specifically to the target audience's needs.

★ WIREFRAMES

Focused on the user onboarding section, homepage section and location section due to time constraints.

★ UI SCREENS

Final visual design for the user onboarding and homepage sections, ensuring consistency and clarity.

★ PROTOTYPE
★ ACCESSIBILITY

A text-based service allows users to receive AAS-friendly location info without a smartphone — bridging the digital divide and serving the next billion users.

★ FUTURE IMPLEMENTATIONS
  • Complete App Design: Remaining sections including location details, ratings, safety, community, and education.
  • Real-Time Location: Accurate, up-to-date AAS-friendly location data.
  • Enhanced Reviews: Helpful ratings, sorting options, and photo uploads.
  • Full Accessibility: Text-to-speech, screen reader, high contrast mode.
  • Usability Testing: Diverse user group testing and iterative improvement.
  • Localisation: Multiple languages and cultural adaptations.
★ MY LEARNINGS

My participation in the Microsoft design competition enhanced my understanding of user-centred design principles and provided recognition for my work. I am grateful for the platform to showcase my solution and engage with the design community.
